02 · The gap

The inside of the plant is missing

Public data shows the water arriving and the water leaving. What the plant did in between, and why, stays inside the utility. That is the main barrier to ML research on treatment, and no catalog can fix it, because there is nothing published to index.

Why it exists, and what would fix it →
Source waterWell coveredRivers, the Delta, reservoirs and groundwater: continuous sensors, decades of samples, satellite bloom indices.
Inside the plantNo open recordDoses, filter head loss, contactor residuals, setpoints, alarms, operator actions, jar tests. Kept in utility historians and logbooks.
Leaving the plantCompliance onlyRegulatory lab results and effluent reports: plentiful, but sampled to prove compliance, not to train models.

03 · Two perspectives

Each field brings half of the work

For water practitioners

What ML can do with your data

  1. Forecast source water hours to days ahead: conductivity, turbidity, algae.
  2. Soft sensors that estimate lab-only values like TOC between grab samples.
  3. Fast surrogates of hydraulic, treatment and biological models for what-if runs.
  4. Early warning of sensor drift and process upsets.
  5. Dose guidance, tested offline or in shadow mode before it ever touches operations.

For ML researchers

What water has already built

  1. Decades of regulated sampling for every public water system and permitted discharger.
  2. Dense sensor networks on rivers and the Delta, with years of 15-minute history.
  3. Mature physics simulators (EPANET, BSM2, PSDM) to generate training data on demand.
  4. Hard constraints: mass balance, kinetics and regulatory limits that a model must respect.
  5. Real stakes: underpredicting a hazard costs far more than average error.

What could be done next

One utility sharing two years of historian data with its event logs and jar tests would close the gap for most of the research directions. Open wastewater SCADA and EPA's 1997–98 plant records let the work start now. See the research directions →

05 · Ground rules

Two tests, kept apart

Copying a simulator and predicting reality are scored separately. A surrogate cannot beat its simulator on reality.

Cheapest model that works

Use the least expensive model that delivers the validated accuracy a decision needs. Sometimes that is an equation.

Hazards scored on their own

Underpredicting a hazard is reported separately from average error.

Operations stay protected

Anything touching live operations runs offline or in shadow mode first.
