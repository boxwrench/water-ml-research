Water Data Atlas: Open Research Data

Keith Wilkinson · title22.org · November 2026 · Working draft. Part of the Water Data Atlas.

Datasets published by universities, research groups, utilities and international programs fill the one hole that government data leaves: treatment-plant process data at minute-to-daily resolution. None of them are Californian, and most come from a single plant, so they suit method development and transfer tests, not California baselines. They also carry the best-documented benchmarks for distribution networks and hydrology. Every row was confirmed through search results only (Nov 2026) unless marked; check license and file contents before use.

Treatment-plant datasets

These are the only open sources of real process data, the kind Lead 5 is after. The Agtrup and Avedøre sets are the strongest: two years at 2-minute resolution, with control variables (doses) separated from outcomes.

Dataset What one row is Coverage Access and license Notes
Agtrup (BlueKolding) WWTP 2-minute SCADA snapshot: control (e.g. chemical dosing), objective (e.g. phosphate) and exogenous (e.g. temperature) variables Aug 2021 to Aug 2023; 125,000 PE plant in Kolding, Denmark (data paper) Mendeley Data CSV, DOI 10.17632/34rpmsxc4z Chemical + biological P removal. Gaps forward-filled by the authors, so mask filled values. Opened in the directions doc
Avedøre WWTP N2O series 2-minute online measurements, 24 signals incl. N2O and DO June 2022 to June 2024, BIOFOS, Copenhagen Data paper on arXiv; check hosting and license Good for soft-sensor and emissions forecasting
Mendeley full-scale DWTP coagulant set One day: raw turbidity, temperature, pH, coagulant dose 1,705 daily records, one unnamed plant CC BY 4.0 The only open drinking-water dose dataset found. Opened in the directions doc
Melbourne Eastern WWTP One day: energy use, hydraulic and biological loads, weather 2014 to 2019 Mendeley Data; built from Melbourne Water open data, which also publishes daily electricity for the plant under CC BY 4.0 (data.vic) Energy-forecasting benchmark
UCI Water Treatment Plant One day of 38 sensor/lab variables across plant stages 527 days, one urban WWTP, from Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona, donated 1993 CC BY 4.0; ucimlrepo id 106 Small and old, but a well-known baseline for fault and state classification

Published studies show that larger drinking-water sets exist but stay private. One settled-turbidity study used 2,527 records from a full-scale conventional plant (McKelvey et al. 2024). Asking authors for data is a cheap lead.

Distribution networks and simulator benchmarks

Real utility network models are rarely shared, so the field runs on benchmark networks plus simulated scenarios. These are Test A data by construction, except BattLeDIM, which mimics a real SCADA record.

Dataset What it holds Size Access Notes
BattLeDIM (L-Town) SCADA from 33 pressure, 3 flow and 1 tank-level sensor on a 42.6 km network serving ~10,000 people, with leak events (rules) 782-node EPANET model; 5-min data Zenodo + GitHub; also loadable through the water_benchmark_hub package (docs) The standard leak-detection benchmark
LeakDB Many simulated leakage scenarios on benchmark networks, plus a scoring algorithm 13.9 GB ZIP Zenodo; generator code on GitHub WNTR-generated; small networks
DiTEC-WDN Simulated hydraulic states for 55 networks 1,000 scenarios per network, ~228 million snapshots Public license per the paper; check the paper for hosting Built for graph neural networks and state estimation
Kentucky hydraulic model database EPANET/KYPIPE model files: literature networks, Battle competitions, US and international city components, synthetic systems Eight groups of networks UKnowledge download, DOI 10.13023/kwrri.wdsrd ASCE task committee repository
Exeter CWS benchmarks Classic design and operation problems with EPANET files and best-known Pareto fronts From the two-loop network up to the 567-variable Exeter network Web download Optimization baselines

Pair any of these with WNTR and EPANET-MSX (see Research Directions) to generate chlorine-residual data for Lead 3.

Hydrology and water-quality benchmarks

Large-sample datasets let a model learn across thousands of sites, which is the cross-system idea behind direction 31. Caravan already includes California basins through CAMELS-US.

Dataset What one row is Coverage Access and license Notes
Caravan Daily streamflow + meteorological forcing + catchment attributes per gauge Merges CAMELS (US and others), HYSETS and more; forcing period 1950 to 2023 since v1.3 (v1.4 notes) Zenodo, netCDF and CSV split into two records since v1.6; code on GitHub The MultiMet extension adds nowcasts and forecasts for 22,492 gauges, CC BY 4.0 (paper)
GEMStat open archive (UNEP GEMS/Water) One freshwater-quality measurement at a station 50M+ measurements, 622 parameters, ~23,000 stations, 42 countries, 1906 to 2024 (v3, Feb 2026) Zenodo ZIP (~201 MB), CC BY 4.0; more data under CC BY-NC via the portal Global river, lake and groundwater chemistry; annual updates
GRQA (Global River Water Quality Archive) Harmonized river chemistry from CESI, GEMStat, GLORICH, Waterbase and the US WQP 1898 to 2020 Data paper with download Older snapshot; useful for cross-continent transfer

For California work, Caravan supplies flow forcing in the same format as other regions. GEMStat and GRQA let a source-water model trained on Delta data be tested abroad.

Utility open-data portals are worth a sweep. NYC and Melbourne Water already publish process-adjacent tables, and some California utilities may have similar portals.

Where to keep looking

Zenodo, Mendeley Data, HydroShare, the Environmental Data Initiative, the Stanford Digital Repository and the UCI repository hold most open water datasets. Search them for "SCADA", "jar test", "filter" and "chlorine residual". Kaggle often re-hosts these without the license, so trace each file back to its original DOI.

What it changes for the project

  • Lead 5 can start before an agreement. Agtrup and Avedøre are 2-minute plant records with doses and outcomes, which is the exact format of the planned utility request. Build loaders and baselines on them now.
  • A drinking-water gap remains. The only open DWTP dose data found is one daily dataset of 1,705 records. Filter run length, head loss and contactor residuals still exist only inside utilities, and in EPA's 1997-98 ICR data (see Federal data).
  • Lead 3 gets real residuals. NYC's distribution samples give measured chlorine to set against WNTR and EPANET-MSX simulations.
  • Watch licenses. WastewaterSCAN and part of GEMStat are non-commercial; Agtrup and Avedøre hosting terms need checking.
