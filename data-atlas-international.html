Water Data Atlas: Other States and Countries

Keith Wilkinson · title22.org · November 2026 · Working draft. Part of the Water Data Atlas.

Outside California the strongest find is South Korea. K-water, the national water corporation, publishes hourly operating data from its treatment plants through open APIs. That is the closest public data to the treatment-plant record that is missing everywhere else (see The gap). England is the other standout: a national water-quality archive with an API, plus near real-time storm overflow data from every water company. Other US states are building catalogs on California's model, but none publishes plant process data. Every row was confirmed through search results only (Nov 2026); check access terms, licence and history before relying on any of it.

South Korea: K-water treatment-plant APIs

K-water reports 394 public datasets (313 files and 81 APIs) on its open data portal and Korea's national public data portal. The APIs need a free service key from data.go.kr, and the documentation is in Korean.

Dataset What one row is Coverage Access Notes
Real-time water quality, hourly One hour at one treatment plant: residual chlorine (mg/L), pH, turbidity (NTU) K-water drinking and industrial plants REST, JSON/XML, data.go.kr key Finished water leaving the plant
Real-time flow, hourly One hour of flow at one intake, plant or booster station, with tag ID and tag description K-water supply system; documentation's sample query is dated 2015-11-20 REST, JSON/XML Tag-level, like a small historian export; flags each value as totalized, difference or instantaneous
Daily raw and settled water quality One day at one regional plant: pH, turbidity, conductivity and alkalinity for raw and settled water; weekly and monthly sets too K-water regional plants REST, JSON/XML Pairs coagulation inputs with a settled-water outcome. No coagulant dose in the documented fields
Sludge production Monthly sludge output per regional plant K-water regional plants REST, JSON/XML A rough proxy for coagulant and solids load
Treatment facility status and intake facility status Capacity, annual and peak production, utilization, energy per unit produced National waterworks, annual CSV download Plant metadata for cross-plant models
Treatment-plant monitoring items The 300 raw and finished-water parameters monitored, with units and test frequency National CSV A data dictionary for the above

Context. K-water's own patented dosing method sets coagulant dose from moving averages of turbidity, temperature, alkalinity, pH, conductivity and TOC, plus an operator adjustment: a ready rules-based baseline for dose models. Its Hwaseong AI water treatment plant (operating since 2022) runs coagulation automatically on big data and a digital twin. Korean full-scale studies already appear in Published Work, but on private data.

What's possible. Multi-plant hourly finished-water quality and flow, plus daily raw versus settled water, give Lead 5 a public warm-up and direction 31 (cross-plant transfer) its first real test set. First step: get a key, pull one plant's full history, and confirm how far back each API goes.

What's possible. Overflow events, river chemistry and, soon, continuous sensors downstream of treatment works make a clean Test B setting for wastewater-to-river forecasting: rainfall and overflow activity in, river ammonia and bacteria out.

Other US states

These follow California's open-data model: catalogs that federate agency data. None publishes treatment-plant process data.

Effort What it is Notes
New Mexico Water Data Initiative Federated water data service under the 2019 Water Data Act; New Mexico was the second state after California to legislate integrated water data Convened by the NM Bureau of Geology and Mineral Resources
Texas Water Data Hub Texas Water Development Board catalog of public water data, launched in beta in 2023 Built on CKAN, like California's portals
Water Data Exchange (WaDE) Western States Water Council program, since 2011, sharing water-rights and water-use data across western states Useful for cross-state demand and allocation work

What it changes for the project

  • The gap narrows, but only in Korea. K-water's hourly plant data is the first public multi-plant treatment record found. It still lacks dose, filter and event data, so Lead 5 remains the critical path.
  • Cross-plant transfer gets a public test. Train on some K-water plants, test on others; then check whether models transfer to Danish or, later, utility data.
  • England is the wastewater-to-river testbed. Its overflow and river data are open, national and near real-time.
  • Other states add catalogs, not process data. Use them for cross-state compliance and supply work.
